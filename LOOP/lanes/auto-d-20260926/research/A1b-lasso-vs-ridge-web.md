# A1b — LASSO vs ridge in GLLVMs: web research

All findings below are UNVERIFIED (web search + abstracts/summaries only; no full-text PDFs read in depth, no code inspected). Confirmed against OpenAlex for title/year/venue/DOI where noted; a few remain unconfirmed (flagged).

## Verdict

Column-wise (group) L1 penalties on the loading matrix Λ — the "ordered factor LASSO" (OFAL) of Hui, Tanaka & Warton (2018, *Biometrics*) — are the one method in the literature purpose-built to answer "how many latent dimensions K" from a single fit: a hierarchically nested group-LASSO penalty shrinks whole columns of Λ to zero in a fixed order, so the number of surviving columns *is* the estimated K, while a per-entry penalty on top gives simple structure within surviving columns. This is qualitatively different from an ordinary ridge/L2 penalty on Λ, which shrinks all loadings continuously toward zero but essentially never sets any exactly to zero and therefore cannot select K; L2 is instead the tool for stabilising runaway or Heywood loadings and is the frequentist mirror of a Gaussian (independent-normal) prior, i.e. plain MAP estimation. The corresponding Bayesian machinery — Bayesian LASSO (Park & Casella 2008/2010, Laplace/double-exponential prior), and, more relevantly for choosing K, the multiplicative gamma process (Bhattacharya & Dunson 2011) and cumulative shrinkage process (Legramanti, Durante & Dunson 2020) — achieves the same "shrink later columns harder" effect through *increasing* shrinkage across columns rather than a hard L1 zero, avoiding rotation and non-smoothness problems but requiring MCMC rather than a single penalized-likelihood/Laplace fit. A real complication for a TMB/Laplace-approximation package: the L1 penalty (and the OFAL group penalty) is non-smooth at zero, which is awkward for a method built on exact gradients and a smooth inner Laplace approximation over random effects — the literature's answers are smoothing approximations to |x|, proximal/coordinate-descent algorithms outside the Laplace loop, or non-convex smooth substitutes (SCAD/MC+, which are themselves non-smooth at zero too) — none of which is a drop-in TMB feature we found evidence of; nobody appears to have published an OFAL-style fit done via TMB's own AD+Laplace machinery specifically. Practically, the field's consensus is: never use naive CV/AIC-style tuning-parameter selection for LASSO-type penalties in latent-variable/SEM contexts without correcting for the selection effect (BIC is common but plain BIC is known to be inconsistent for this purpose; ERIC (Hui, Warton & Foster) and adapted/EBIC variants exist precisely to fix this), and to refit unpenalized (or "relaxed") at the selected sparsity/K to remove L1 shrinkage bias before reporting effect sizes, treating any inference following selection as invalid unless a selective-inference correction is applied.

## LASSO vs ridge in GLLVMs — comparison table

| Property | LASSO (L1, entrywise or grouped) | Ridge (L2) |
|---|---|---|
| Sets loadings exactly to zero? | Yes — entrywise LASSO zeros individual λ_jk; **group/column LASSO (OFAL) zeros whole columns**, which is what selects K | No — shrinks continuously, essentially never exactly zero |
| Selects K (number of latent dims)? | Only the *group/ordered* column-wise variant (OFAL) does this directly, in one fit; entrywise LASSO alone does not | No — L2 does not zero columns; K must still be chosen by other means (fit K=1..Kmax + IC, as in current `gllvm`) |
| Rotation-invariant? | No — L1 on Λ (fixing element-wise sparsity) is **not** invariant to rotations of the latent space, so the penalty actively interacts with (and can conflict with) the lower-triangular identifiability constraint used in TMB/gllvm-style fits; this is a real methodological tension flagged in the sparse-FA literature (rotation techniques vs. penalization: Hirose & Yamamoto explicitly frame penalization as *generalizing* rotation) | Yes if applied as an isotropic penalty on the whole Λ (∑λ_jk²) — invariant under orthogonal rotation of columns, so it does not fight the identifiability constraint the way L1 does |
| Bayesian equivalent | Laplace/double-exponential prior (Park & Casella); MGPS and CUSP are the natural GLLVM-flavoured *increasing-shrinkage* analogues used for K-selection rather than plain Bayesian LASSO | Independent Gaussian (normal) prior on loadings — ordinary ridge = Gaussian MAP, standard in Bayesian GLLVM/factor-analysis priors for stabilising loadings |
| Bias | Yes — L1 shrinks retained (nonzero) coefficients too, a known bias the relaxed-LASSO / post-selection-refit literature exists to fix (Meinshausen's relaxed LASSO: refit least-squares within the selected support) | Yes, but "all-shrink" bias rather than selection bias; typically judged acceptable since the goal is stabilisation, not selection |
| Valid inference after fitting? | No — naive SEs/CIs after LASSO selection are invalid; requires selective-inference correction or refit-and-treat-as-fixed-model (with attendant "look, we then pretend the selection didn't happen" caveat) | Yes, in the ordinary ridge sense (shrunk but not model-selecting), though ridge SEs still require care (they are for a different, biased estimator) |
| Compatible with TMB/Laplace as-is? | Problematic — L1 (and OFAL's group-L1) is **non-smooth at zero**, which is awkward for TMB's exact-AD + smooth-inner-Laplace-approximation machinery; literature solutions are smoothing approximations to \|x\|, proximal/coordinate-descent steps run outside the Laplace loop, or non-convex smooth-ish substitutes (SCAD/MC+, themselves non-smooth at 0) — **no confirmed prior use of OFAL-style fitting inside TMB found in this search** | Yes, cleanly — an L2 penalty is just an additional smooth quadratic term (equivalently a Gaussian random-effect prior on Λ), which is exactly the kind of term TMB/Laplace already handles natively (this is effectively what a "Λ as random effect with normal prior" formulation already gives) |

## How LASSO should be used (practical recipe from the literature)

1. **Use a group/ordered penalty on Λ columns, not an entrywise one, if the goal is choosing K.** Entrywise LASSO alone gives sparse loadings but does not remove whole factors; only a column-grouped penalty (Yuan & Lin 2006 group-LASSO logic, as adapted into GLLVMs by Hui, Tanaka & Warton 2018's OFAL) can zero out an entire latent dimension in one fit. [Hui, Tanaka & Warton 2018; Yuan & Lin 2006]
2. **Do not tune with plain CV/AIC.** In penalized latent-variable/SEM settings the literature (regsem/lslx ecosystem) commonly defaults to BIC for choosing the penalty strength, but purpose-built alternatives exist because BIC's usual justification assumes unpenalized MLE: ERIC (Hui, Warton & Foster; extended in JASA 2015 for adaptive LASSO) explicitly corrects the model-complexity penalty for the effect of shrinkage, and is selection-consistent in regimes where plain BIC is not. [Jacobucci et al. JASA 2015 summary; regsem/lslx documentation]
3. **Refit unpenalized (or "relaxed") once K/sparsity is chosen**, to remove the L1 shrinkage bias in the surviving loadings, following the same logic as Meinshausen's relaxed LASSO (φ=0 refit = unpenalized least squares on the selected support). This is the standard move to separate the *selection* step (find K, find nonzero entries) from the *estimation* step (get unbiased loadings given that structure). [Meinshausen relaxed LASSO]
4. **Treat post-selection inference as invalid unless corrected.** SEs/CIs computed naively after a LASSO-driven K or entry selection do not have their nominal coverage; the field's answer is either selective inference machinery or an explicit refit-and-condition-on-selection stance, never silent pass-through of standard TMB-style asymptotic SEs from the penalized fit. [general LASSO post-selection inference literature — not GLLVM-specific, flagged as generic statistical knowledge rather than a single located GLLVM paper]
5. **Expect non-smoothness at the identifiability boundary to bite in a Laplace/TMB context.** Because L1 is non-differentiable at 0 and the lower-triangular lower-dimension constraint already sits at a non-smooth boundary (a column *being* zero is itself the K-selection event), any TMB implementation needs either (a) a smoothed |x| surrogate for the outer penalty (leaving the inner Laplace approximation over random effects untouched), (b) a proximal/coordinate-descent step wrapped around otherwise-ordinary TMB calls, or (c) reparametrisation so that the penalty acts on a smooth transform. No paper found in this search demonstrates a working combination of OFAL-style group-L1 with a TMB Laplace fit specifically — this is a genuine methodological gap for our packages, not a solved recipe to copy. [inference from general non-smooth-optimization-in-TMB search; no direct citation for the combination]
6. **If rotation-invariance matters more than exact sparsity, prefer ridge (or a Bayesian Gaussian-column prior) over LASSO for stabilisation, and reserve LASSO/group-LASSO specifically for the K-selection question.** Because L1 breaks rotation invariance and interacts with the lower-triangular constraint, mixing "stabilise Heywood loadings" (ridge's job) and "select K" (LASSO's job) in a single undifferentiated penalty risks solving neither cleanly. [inference from Hirose & Yamamoto's rotation-vs-penalization framing + general L1/L2 properties]

## Risks for our packages (gllvmTMB / GLLVMModels.jl)

- The one method purpose-built for "never ask the user for K" (OFAL) has **no confirmed existing software implementation** we could locate (not in `gllvm`, not a standalone package) — building it would mean implementing the group-LASSO-in-TMB combination essentially from scratch, including solving the non-smoothness-at-zero problem for the AD/Laplace machinery. This is a real, currently-unsolved engineering problem, not a "just add a penalty term" change.
- L1 on Λ is not rotation-invariant and therefore actively fights the lower-triangular identifiability constraint our TMB models already impose; naively adding an entrywise or column L1 penalty on top of the existing constrained parametrisation could produce penalty-vs-constraint conflicts (e.g. the constraint forcing a particular entry to be exactly the free diagonal/lower-triangular scale parameter while the penalty simultaneously tries to shrink it to zero).
- The literature's Bayesian alternatives (MGPS, CUSP) that *are* purpose-built for column-count selection are MCMC-native and do not have an obvious Laplace-approximation/TMB analogue; adapting the "increasing shrinkage across columns" idea to a smooth, differentiable penalty compatible with Laplace would itself be a novel contribution, not a known recipe.
- Tuning-parameter selection conventions in the adjacent SEM literature (BIC, ERIC) were developed for standard (non-latent-variable, non-Laplace-approximated) penalized likelihood; whether they transfer validly to a TMB-fitted GLLVM (where the marginal likelihood is itself an approximation) is unverified and worth flagging as an open question rather than assuming it carries over.
