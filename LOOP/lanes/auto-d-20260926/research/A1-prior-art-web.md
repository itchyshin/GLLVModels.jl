# A1 — Prior art: choosing the number of latent variables (d / K) in GLLVMs / JSDMs

Status: UNVERIFIED (web-sourced via WebSearch/WebFetch + OpenAlex-style bibliographic checks; not yet checked by a person against full-text PDFs). Budget ~25 min; not exhaustive.

## Verdict

Yes, this has been worked on, but mostly from two directions that rarely meet: (1) the ecology/JSDM side (gllvm, boral, HMSC, glmmTMB `rr()`) treats it as an ordinary model-selection problem — refit across a grid of d and pick by AIC/BIC or cross-validated deviance — with almost no simulation evidence on whether this recovers the *true* d in non-Gaussian, non-Gaussian-link GLLVMs specifically; and (2) the statistics/econometrics side (Chen & Li 2022 Biometrika; Kidziński et al. 2022 JMLR "GMF"; classical Bai & Ng 2002, Ahn & Horenstein 2013) treats it as a high-dimensional rank/factor-number estimation problem with actual consistency proofs and simulation recovery tables, but for count/binary generalized factor models this is recent (2020–2022) and not yet the default in the applied ecology packages. The clearest gap: none of the applied GLLVM/JSDM packages (gllvm, boral, HMSC, glmmTMB `rr()`) ship a validated, simulation-checked default rule for choosing d — they all defer to "try a few values of d and compare AIC/BIC/cross-validated deviance," a rule inherited from Gaussian factor analysis without checked non-Gaussian recovery guarantees. Classical factor-analysis theory (Hayashi/Bentler/Yuan 2007; Drton 2009) further warns that the naive likelihood-ratio route to choosing the number of factors is invalid because the null distribution is non-standard at the boundary/singularity — a caveat that carries over to GLLVMs but is rarely mentioned in the ecological literature. Post-selection inference after choosing d is essentially absent for GLLVMs specifically (only general post-selection-inference theory for regression/Lasso settings was found).

## What the main packages do by default

- **gllvm (R)**: user specifies `num.lv`; standard practice is to fit a small range of candidate values and compare by AIC/BIC (documented in vignettes as an available approach), not an automatic data-driven default. No built-in automatic-K selector confirmed.
- **boral (R)**: same pattern — `lv.control$num.lv` set by the user; no automatic selection found.
- **HMSC**: uses latent factors at multiple hierarchical levels (sample, site, etc.); earlier Bayesian implementations used adaptive-shrinkage-style priors (in the spirit of Bhattacharya & Dunson) to let the effective number of active factors be data-driven rather than fixed by AIC/BIC refitting, but the exact mechanism in the current `Hmsc` R package was not directly confirmed from the fetched material (LEAD).
- **sjSDM**: does *not* use a low-rank latent-variable approximation at all by design — it fits a full covariance matrix in the likelihood via Monte Carlo simulation, sidestepping the "choose d" problem entirely (confirmed from search summary of the package vignette/CRAN page).
- **VAST**: no confirmed information found on its default number-of-factors rule (absence — see below).
- **glmmTMB `rr()`**: defaults to d = 2 for the reduced-rank structure; guidance is to refit across a grid of d and select by AIC or cross-validation (confirmed from arXiv:2411.04411 fetch above); no simulation-validated automatic selector.
- **gllvmTMB**: not found in any search result — absence (see below).

## Recovery evidence summary

- **Strongest, most directly relevant hit**: Chen & Li (2022, Biometrika) — a genuine simulation study of factor-number recovery in a *generalized* (binary/count) high-dimensional latent factor model, with near-perfect recovery once J ≥ 300 and N ≥ J, degrading at small J or with weak factors/missingness. This is the closest thing found to "how well does this work" for GLLVM-like models.
- Kidziński et al. (2022) propose cross-validated deviance for rank selection but the fetched excerpts did not surface a rank-recovery simulation table — needs a full-text read to confirm whether one exists (LEAD).
- No recovery simulation was found for gllvm's own `num.lv`/AIC practice, for boral, for HMSC's factor count, or for glmmTMB's `rr()` — i.e., the packages ecologists actually use lack published recovery evidence, in contrast to the more theoretical statistics papers (Chen & Li) which do have it but for a narrower/idealized generalized factor model, not the full GLLVM-with-covariates setting ecologists fit.

## LRT / chi-bar caveat evidence

- Hayashi, Bentler & Yuan (2007, Structural Equation Modeling 14:505-526): shows the likelihood-ratio test for the number of factors in exploratory FA does **not** follow the nominal chi-square distribution when the hypothesized number of factors is at or exceeds the boundary of identifiability, because of rank-deficiency/non-identifiability — a key reason naive LRT-based selection of the number of factors "overfactors."
- Drton (2009, Annals of Statistics 37:979-1012), "Likelihood ratio tests and singularities": general theory showing LRTs near singular/boundary points of the parameter space (factor analysis with one factor is the worked example) have non-standard limiting distributions connected to eigenvalues of Wishart matrices, not a simple chi-square. This directly underwrites why AIC/BIC (which assume regular asymptotics) are also suspect as naive selectors for the number of latent factors, and why none of the recovery-simulation papers above rely on a plain LRT.
- Neither caveat paper is GLLVM-specific; both are classical (Gaussian) factor-analysis theory, but the boundary/non-identifiability mechanism they describe applies structurally to any latent-factor model, including GLLVMs.

## Post-selection inference

- No paper specifically addressing post-selection inference for the *number of latent variables* in a GLLVM/JSDM was found.
- General post-selection-inference literature located (Berk et al.-style family-wise control; selective inference conditioning on the selection event; "post-double-selection" for treatment effects) is all in regression/Lasso variable-selection settings, not factor-number selection. This is an **absence** (see below), not just an unresolved lead — it appears to be genuinely unaddressed in the GLLVM literature.
